# 2026-10-04 — The 2IC (`0143`): the third configured role, and why she keeps the volunteer floor

`docs/roles-and-permissions.md` §12 R4. It follows `2026-10-04-medical-role.md` and
`2026-10-04-maintenance-role.md` (the role row, the scopes, `home_path = '/home'`, a job in `jobs.ts`,
`bundleOfRole()` checked against `role_permissions`) and does not repeat them. This file is the one call this
stream existed to make, what the four screens turned out to need, and a per-screen handover for Management (R5).

## 1. The call: `legacy_role = 'volunteer'`, not `staff`

The two inputs conflicted, as the brief said. #359 wrote *"for the 2IC it is the wrong floor, since hers is
staff-wide, so decide there whether she borrows `staff`"*; Lutan's later ruling the same day was *three tiles,
everything else `None`, widen when someone hits a wall*. The newer ruling wins, and it settles it:

- **`staff` as the floor** would let the role-named policies admit her to everything staff reads: every
  resident, the whole medical record, contacts, the translations table. Her cells would say four jobs and the
  database would say a staff member, and the cells would stop being the guard. "Everything else None" would be
  true only in the app, which is exactly what R1 (`0134`) spent a batch undoing.
- **`volunteer` as the floor** keeps the cells the whole grant. The cost is real and is paid in `0143`: every
  stock table answers by role name, so each needed a view, a policy or a guard that asks the cell. The shape was
  the same as R2's and R3's, only larger (§3).

`volunteer` is therefore the right floor for all three configured roles, and the cost of narrowness is a
migration, once, per role. Her row: `second_in_command`, **2IC** / รองผู้จัดการ (Lutan to confirm the Thai; L4),
`kind = 'custom'`, scopes copied from R3 (`scope_clinical = any` for the reason R3 gave).

**What the staff floor would have bought, so nobody is surprised.** Two things that work for a staff login and do
not for her are in §5. Neither is a reason to widen her floor; both are R5's.

## 2. Her cells and her four jobs

`stock.count`, `stock.purchasing`, `stock.delivery` (all Yes), `maintenance.jobs` Edit, `maintenance.progress`,
`recurring.do_own`, `resident.record` Read, `facility.enclosures` Read. Not given: `stock.usage`,
`stock.medications`, `stock.diets`, `stock.correct`, `recurring.manage` (P2), photos on a job. Four jobs in
`jobs.ts`: Do Stocktaking, Do the Purchasing, Record a Delivery, Do Maintenance (R3's job, reused as written).

**The dependency question the backlog asked.** *"Purchasing needs the last count, the receipts since and
`safety_stock`."* No activity exists for *reading* stock, and adding one is a catalogue change this stream was
told not to make. So the dependency is not listed in the bundle (listing `stock.count` under Purchasing would make
the jobs inseparable: Purchasing would grant counting). It lives where the data is read: the two views, `stock_counts`,
`item_unit_conversions` and `stock_receipts` answer **any one** of the stock cells, the receipts the two that read
them. `scripts/check-2ic-role.mjs` proves it with three roles built inside its transaction, holding
`stock.purchasing`, `stock.count` and `stock.delivery` alone: each reads every figure its own job needs and is
refused the writes of the others. So the union is not a partition, and removing a job takes away only the
activity that job alone granted.

## 3. What the four screens needed beyond the cells (the departures)

R2 and R3 told the next stream to expect this; it was the same again, with three things that were new.

| What | Why | The fix |
|---|---|---|
| `medication`, `diet_types` | role-named; the tables also carry `cost_per_unit` (and the diets' daily quantities), which a policy cannot hide | `stock_medications` and `stock_diet_types`: fixed-column views **without** the price, rows for any of the three stock cells. All three screens read the views, **for every role** (admin, management and staff read the same columns they used; none of their answers changed) |
| `contacts` (the supplier) | role-named; a policy would expose phone and address | `stock_vendors`: id, name, archived |
| `stock_counts`, `item_unit_conversions`, `stock_receipts` | role-named | select policies on the cells; insert and delete on receipts for `stock.delivery`. Update is not given: a delivery typed wrong is deleted and recorded again |
| `record_stocktake()` | the guard named admin, management, staff | also admits a holder of `stock.count` (same pattern as `record_recurring_job()` in `0141`) |
| the forecast behind Purchasing | **new.** `medication_forecast()` and `diet_forecast()` are security invoker, read prescriptions and diets she must not see, and **go through `public.resident_current_state`, which answers nothing to a volunteer floor** | `stock_medication_forecast()` and `stock_diet_forecast()`: definer **copies of the two queries** over `private.resident_current_state`, behind `stock.purchasing`; the diet one has no cost column |
| `stock_receipts_stamp()` | **new.** The trigger that copies an item's unit onto a delivery read `medication` as the caller and found nothing | now security definer, same body. `recorded_by` is still the caller (`auth.uid()` is a claim) |
| the photo route | **new.** A medication label is served only if the caller can select the row | also tries `stock_medications` |

**Two of these were found only by signing in as her in the browser, and the checks had passed.** The forecast
wrapper first *wrapped* the original and returned zero rows for her: the check only counted that the call was
allowed. And the unit came back null on a delivery she recorded: the check only counted that the insert worked.
Both probes now assert the content (`check-2ic-role.mjs` compares the wrappers with management's originals, and
the insert probe requires the stamped unit). **Lesson for R5: a probe that asks "did it error?" passes on a
silently empty result. Where a volunteer-based role reads through a definer function, assert what comes back.**
The forecast copies can drift from the originals; the comparison is what stops that.

**`0143` was edited after it had been applied to dev** (the wrapper rewrite and the stamp trigger), the one time
this repo's "applied files are never edited" is not followed. It is unmerged, I hold the migration slot, nothing
but dev had it, and the tool checks only against `origin/main`'s copy. The corrected sections were re-run on dev
by hand and the dev database equals the file; production has never seen either version.

## 5. Left alone, found on the way (reported, not fixed)

- **A recurring job linked to `/stocktake` cannot be given to her through the rota picker, and it loses its link
  on her `/my`.** This is the `legacy_role` bridge R3 named, and the weekly stocktake is the case that matters most
  (P4). The picker filters people by the enum role (`app_users.role` = `volunteer` for her) and asks the database
  whether *that role* may open the page; a volunteer may not. `canDoJob` and `ASSIGNABLE_ROLES` need to work in role
  *keys*, and `app_users` needs to expose the key. It works today for a staff login and not for her, which is the
  real price of §1. It belongs with R5's recurring-jobs-on-a-phone work; filed on the backlog branch. Until then
  Management cannot assign her the weekly stocktake from the picker, and a task assigned to her by another route can
  still be marked done on `/my`.
- **The maintenance board still offers photos and Delete job**, as in #359. Delete has no activity of its own (it
  stays admin, management and staff by role name) and photos are `maintenance.photos`, not hers. Nothing was
  gated, to stay out of the board's files this batch; the database refuses both, and the delete message reads
  oddly. The decision is Management's: P7 says she creates maintenance tasks *along with* the Director.
- **The Head of Medical's label photos** probably have the same blank (the proxy asks the `medication` table; her
  list reads `medication_list_medications`). Not checked, not changed.
- **`check-permission-tables.mjs` was already red on `main`** after `0136`/`0141`: its baseline counted the roles'
  own seed inserts as unexplained audit rows (21 against an expected 0). Fixed here by excluding `INSERT`s by no actor
  on `roles` and `role_permissions`; it asserts the same thing about everything else.
